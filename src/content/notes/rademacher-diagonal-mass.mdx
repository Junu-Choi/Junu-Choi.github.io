## One identity

Estimate the trace of a nonzero real symmetric matrix $A$ by averaging $z^\top A z$. For independent standard Gaussian or Rademacher ($\pm1$) entries, the single-probe variances satisfy

$$
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{Rad}}}{\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{Gauss}}}
= 1-\rho,
\qquad
\rho = \frac{\sum_i A_{ii}^2}{\lVert A\rVert_F^2}.
$$

Both estimators are unbiased. Since $z_i^2=1$, Rademacher probes remove the diagonal's contribution to variance. The relative saving is exactly $\rho$.

## What the experiment shows

For the input Hessians of the tested smooth, fully connected networks:

- **Random initialisation:** at dimension 512, $\rho$ is 0.28–0.54% across tanh, GELU, and softplus. The variance saving is correspondingly small.
- **After fitting an additive target:** at dimension 64, $\rho$ reaches 0.89. Rademacher probes then need about **nine times fewer samples for the same mean-squared error**. The generic-target control stays near 0.07.
